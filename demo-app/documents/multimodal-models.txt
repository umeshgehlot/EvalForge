Multimodal Language Models

Multimodal language models process and generate content across multiple modalities — typically text combined with images, audio, or video. They extend the capabilities of pure text LLMs to understand visual content and reason about the physical world.

Vision-language models (VLMs) accept both text and image inputs. The dominant architecture encodes images with a vision encoder (typically a vision Transformer like CLIP or ViT), projects the visual features into the language model's embedding space using a learned projection layer, and concatenates image tokens with text tokens as input to the language model. The language model then attends across both visual and textual tokens.

CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining, OpenAI 2021) trained a dual encoder on 400 million image-text pairs using a contrastive objective: matching images to their captions in embedding space. CLIP's image encoder is widely used as a vision backbone for multimodal models.

LLaVA (Large Language and Vision Assistant) combined CLIP with Vicuna (a LLaMA variant) and a simple linear projection. Despite its simplicity, it demonstrated strong visual understanding. Later versions added better vision encoders and more sophisticated projectors.

GPT-4V (GPT-4 with Vision) demonstrated frontier multimodal capability: reading text in images, understanding charts, interpreting medical scans, writing code from UI screenshots, and solving visual math problems. The architecture details were not disclosed.

Claude's vision capability supports document understanding, chart interpretation, code extraction from screenshots, and detailed image description. Gemini 1.5 extended multimodal reasoning to video understanding across long context windows.

Audio models process speech natively. Whisper (OpenAI) is a robust speech recognition model. GPT-4o processes audio, text, and images natively in a unified model rather than chaining separate systems, enabling faster and more natural voice interactions.

Evaluation of multimodal models uses benchmarks like VQA (Visual Question Answering), MME (Multimodal Evaluation), MMMU (Massive Multitask Multimodal Understanding), and ChartQA. Hallucination is particularly prevalent in multimodal models — models confidently describe objects that are not present in images.
